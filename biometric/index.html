How it works, and what it will not tell you

      

        
Heart rate
          
Each heartbeat pushes a little more blood into the skin of your face, which changes its colour by a fraction of a percent. The page averages the colour of your forehead and cheeks (the boxes drawn on the picture) about 30 times a second and looks for a steady rhythm between 42 and 180 beats a minute.

          
Noise always has some strongest rhythm, so one stretch of video is not enough. A number appears only when the same rhythm shows up in separate ten-second stretches, or stands out overwhelmingly in one. That takes 15 to 30 seconds of sitting still in decent light. Until then the panel says what it is waiting for. Point it at a photograph and it should never produce a number.

          
Check it against a watch or a finger on your wrist. This is a demonstration, not a medical device.

        

        
Expression
          
The face tracker measures about fifty muscle movements, such as lip corners pulling up or brows lowering. The bars show seven of them, measured against your own resting face. Four expressions get a name when their textbook movements clearly win: happy, surprised, angry and sad. Anything else is shown as neutral or unclear.

          
It reads what your face is doing, not what you feel. Lowered brows read as angry whether you are cross or just concentrating. Fear and disgust are left out because this tracker cannot tell them apart from other expressions reliably.

        

        
Tiredness
          
It watches your eyelids and jaw for the signs used in driver-drowsiness research: the share of time your eyes are at least 80% closed over the last minute (under 8% is normal, 15% or more is drowsy), eye closures lasting half a second or longer, and yawns. It needs 30 seconds of seeing your eyes before it gives a verdict.

          
These are visible signs over the last minute. It cannot know how tired you feel or how you slept, and blink rate alone means little, since people blink less when reading a screen.

        

        
What was left out
          
An earlier version of this page also showed age, gender, stress, a wellness score, skin analysis, breathing rate and heart-rate variability. They were dropped because a webcam and a face tracker cannot measure them reliably:

          
    
            
  • Age and gender were guessed from face proportions, which do not determine either.
    • 
            
  • Stress and wellness were formulas stacked on other shaky numbers.
    • 
            
  • Heart-rate variability needs beat timing far finer than 30 frames a second gives.
    • 
            
  • Breathing can sometimes be seen in the shoulders, but not dependably from a face.
    • 
          

        

        
Privacy
          
The camera picture is processed on your own device. The page downloads the face-tracking model from Google and then sends nothing back: no video, no pictures, no measurements. Nothing is stored, and closing the tab ends it.
